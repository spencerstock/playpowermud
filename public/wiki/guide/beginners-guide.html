What Powermud is

Powermud is a fantasy LPMud that has been running since 1993. You play it by typing commands in a text world of cities, dungeons and wilderness, with two dozen guilds, professions, magic and other players.

  • Server: powermud.com port 2000 (telnet, no SSL)
  • Clients: Any MUD client works, for example Mudlet, MUSHclient, TinTin++ or Blightmud.
  • New account: Type a new user name at the login prompt, then create <name>. Names are letters only.
  • Fresh start: Every old character was archived on 25 Oct 2026, so everyone is new.
  • Colour: Turn ANSI on. If tells and says look wrong, use eset to fix the colours.

Your first hour

  1. Create your character. Choose a race that fits the guild you want later (see Which race for which guild). 'none' is accepted for the real name and email questions.
  2. Gear up with Pete. You start in the Newbie Arena. say equip me gets you a torch, leather armour, a small dagger, mace and sword, and 50 drakaan.
  3. Wear and wield two weapons. wear all, then wield two weapons. Sword trains slash, mace blunt, dagger pierce. Pick the skill your future guild keeps.
  4. Get blessed. say bless me. Ask again when you see 'You lose your blessed feeling.' Use say heal me when you're hurt. You have to say these.
  5. Fight rats. Go north and kill rat. Afterwards, get all from corpse and optionally bury corpse.
  6. Train. skills shows your skills, cost shows prices and train all at Pete raises them. A new character can go from 1 to almost 500 exp in about 20 minutes.
  7. Try the Academy. The PowerMud Academy, down and north from Pete, gives free exp and coins.
  8. Leave at 500 exp. At 500 exp the arena moves you out. Use restat first if you want it, then find a guild.
Don't fight outside the arena yet. Brand-new characters who fight outside the arena will probably be killed.

Reading your prompt

An example prompt: [HP: 600(600(100%)) SP: 62(200(31%)) XP: 21270(730(36%)) Cash: 885(29370)]

  • HP: Current (max (percent)). Constitution and total exp raise your max.
  • SP: Spell or skill points. Openers like assassinate cost 5. Below about 3 you get 'too fatigued', so rest.
  • XP: Total experience, followed by a progress counter that shrinks as you gain exp. Total exp decides your title, max HP, guild joining (500), item save levels (1,000 for magic gear) and homeland (4,000 or less).
  • Cash: Drakaan you're carrying (drakaan in the bank)

  • Human — Average at everything and the widest guild choice of any race (13 guilds).
  • Elf — Good and nature-loving; home city Elinost and the Great Forest. Natural infravision. Joins the Elite, Priests of Eli, Bastion and Rangers.
  • Half-Elf — Mixes with both races; the second-widest guild list (10).
  • Drow — Purely evil Underdark elves of Nal Shaddar who worship Loth and hate elves. They see in the dark without light, and drow belong to noble houses (see Drow). Guilds split by gender: males Melee-Magthere, females Arach-Tillith.
  • Gnome — Tinkers and gem-cutters with very high illusion resistance; natural Illusionists.
  • Svirfneblin — Deep gnomes of the Underdark, good miners, mostly with illusionist talent.
  • Dwarf — Stout warriors who love gold and hate orcs and goblins. Natural Stronghold Honor Guard, also Church of Kertaine.
  • Duergar — Evil, half-mad Underdark dwarves of Dunkrist, sometimes allied with drow. Homeguard or Dark Trinity.
  • Halfling — High Dex makes picking locks, pockets and hiding easy. Thief, Assassin, Merchant, Church or Rangers.
  • Half-Orc — Strong outcasts. Militia, Battlemages, Cult or Assassin.
  • Half-Ogre — The strongest and toughest race, with the weakest minds; tends toward evil. Militia, Cult, Disciples, Thief or Assassin.
  • Minotaur — Sea-faring bull-men. Only special helmets fit over the horns. Mariner is their own guild; Battlemages also take them.
  • Infravision: elves have it naturally, and Underdark races (drow, duergar, svirfneblin) see better in the dark than an elf. The Alteration spell Infravision gives elf-level dark vision to anyone.
  • Alignment is Good, Neutral or Evil and shifts with what you kill: killing something of your own alignment pushes you toward the opposite. score shows it. Monks are strongest when neutral.
  • Some cities' NPCs attack certain races on sight, so check how a city treats your race before walking in.

  • Hometowns: Kalinport, Deepwater and Threshold, plus Jaarmin Keep, Dunagin and Zahkar for advanced players. Pick one near your guild, because homeland takes you there. Some hometowns grant special abilities, and you can change hometown later.

Guilds

Join at 500+ exp (Merchants are the exception). At the recruiter, type info for the joining task. Guilds lists every guild the same way: which races can join, the minimum stats, where it is, and a short note from in-game help.

Skills don't always carry over. Some skills stop working when you join a guild. Check help <guild> before you train a weapon type in the arena.

Professions

Once you're established in a guild you can learn professions: specialisations that add or boost skills, sometimes at a cost to others. Lower professions are easier; each upper profession needs a related lower one first. Every profession has its own help file.

Combat basics

  • Fights go in rounds. With two weapons you swing twice, and a health meter shows after each round.
  • Matchup lines, from easiest to hardest: 'They can't seem to hit you!', 'You should win with some extra effort', 'very close, but you should win', 'It is even!', 'fairly close, but you should lose', 'You should lose in a breakneck speed'.
  • gauge rates an NPC's skills (from 'perfect' down to 'almost nonexistent'), not its danger, so don't use it to pick targets.
  • Assassins open with hide, then assassinate <mob>. 'It seems that you are being observed' means wait and hide again.
  • 'thoroughly bored with hiding' only blocks hiding in that room, so move on.
  • Watch group size. Myconids and kobold villagers help each other, so start with mobs that are alone.
  • Mostly evil NPCs attack on sight; good ones usually don't.
  • NPCs come back on 15-minute marks of the MUD's uptime clock, not 15 minutes after you kill them.
  • Your gear wears down ('Your shortsword takes some damage'), so repair at a smith now and then.

  • A good rule: leave and heal at 55% HP, don't start a new fight under about 70%, and top up to 95% before a hard area.
  • Set wimpy to 40%. It runs on the game's side, so it can still save you if your connection drops.
  • 'Your stomach rumbles' means you're hungry. Eating at a waiter fixes it.

Death and getting lost

  • When you die you become a ghost. pray brings you back, and then you can go back and loot your corpse.
  • Quest progress resets when you die or log out, so finish a quest in one session.
  • In the dark, light a torch, wait for daytime (time) or use homeland (4,000 exp or less).
  • Many road rooms share the same description, especially on the Threshold to Kalinport road. Count your moves and look for landmarks.
  • Squirrels on the road can trip you ('tripping you!'), and that move doesn't happen. That throws off a long alias, so check where you ended up.

Money, selling and gear

  • Loot every corpse with get all from corpse. To sell things lying on the floor, use get all, then sell all.
  • Threshold's general shop is north of Frontiers Street (one east of the square, then north). The keeper keeps hours, so come back later if he's away.
  • In Kalinport, Collin Grokker's smithy buys gear and repairs.
  • In Nal Shaddar, the general Shopkeeper buys loot and the Gem Dealer just south of him buys gems.
  • Each town has a bank where you can store your money.
  • Magic items need 1,000 exp before you can use them or keep them through a logout. A bag keeps your inventory tidy. Use secure on anything you'd hate to lose.
  • High Charisma gets better prices when haggling, and Merchants sell at much better rates than anyone else.

Community and etiquette

  • The admins are active and helpful: they write the news, help with houses and guilds, and shout in chat. Keep replies to admins short and polite.
  • who shows who's online, including staff.
  • Regular players share tips freely and sometimes give away gear or cast buffs on newer players.
  • Player-killing is opt-in.
  • Don't share quest walkthroughs, which the rules forbid. Don't type commands other players ask you to type, or give away items or account details.
